Where the Data Lives

Six public databases that underpin modern genetics research. This site draws on all of them — from variant registration to population frequencies to clinical interpretation.

Every rsID, allele frequency, and clinical significance rating you see on this site originates in one of these databases. They are free, continuously maintained by research institutions and government bodies, and together form the backbone of how modern genetics is read and published.

NCBI dbSNP ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp

The authoritative registry for every known SNP. Run by the National Center for Biotechnology Information, it assigns each variant its rsID — the universal naming system used across all genetics research globally. The source of allele frequencies, consequence annotations (synonymous, missense, stop-gained), and chromosomal coordinates.

SNPedia snpedia.com

A community-maintained wiki of clinical SNP interpretations. Each rsID page carries a magnitude score (0–10) summarising the strength of associated evidence, links to supporting studies, and plain-language summaries of what the variant is thought to do. Less formal than ClinVar, but often more readable — and frequently updated faster.

rs2236225 — example SNP ↗
GeneCards genecards.org

A gene-centric portal from the Weizmann Institute of Science. Each card aggregates data from over 150 sources into one page — known variants, protein structure, expression patterns by tissue, disease associations, drug interactions, and pathway memberships. A good first stop when you want the full picture around a specific gene rather than a specific variant.

gnomAD gnomad.broadinstitute.org

The Genome Aggregation Database, produced by the Broad Institute. Contains whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing data from over 125,000 individuals across diverse ancestry groups — making it the gold standard for population-level allele frequency data. Particularly valuable for understanding how rare or common a variant is in specific ancestral populations, and whether it's ever been observed in a healthy cohort.

gnomad.broadinstitute.org ↗
OMIM omim.org

Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man — the authoritative catalogue of human genes and genetic disorders, continuously updated since 1966. Each entry links a gene or locus to its known phenotypic effects, with evidence classifications and references to the original discovery literature. The bedrock resource for understanding gene–disease relationships.

omim.org ↗
ClinVar ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar

NCBI's database of clinically interpreted variants, aggregating submissions from diagnostic laboratories, research groups, and clinicians worldwide. Each variant receives a clinical significance rating — pathogenic, likely pathogenic, benign, likely benign, or uncertain — based on the cumulative weight of evidence submitted. Essential for understanding whether a specific variant has a formally recognised disease connection.
